\section{Conclusion}
VL-MOF provides a mechanized, reusable structural interpretation of EMOF 2.5.1.
The formalization separates structural multiplicity from class-creation bounds,
occurrence counts from container identities, and parent uniqueness from active
container-role uniqueness. Inherited declaration identity and opposite-count
consequences apply across supported metamodels. Checker correctness and conditional
source adequacy connect practical checking and authoring to that foundation.
The published Train projection makes concrete both structural consequences and
the limits of what acceptance establishes.

The EMF comparison distinguishes common outcomes, schema restrictions and
validator differences. Repeated timings cover two small aligned states; the
larger Train input exposes a default-presence mismatch.

The next research steps have specific dependencies. Reflective operations can
reuse identities, occurrence typing and conformance, but require transition
relations and operation-specific preservation or failure results. Defaults and
unset histories require a richer observation state; additional value domains
require their own equality and typing account. Metadata interpretation must extend
the bounded pilot with packages, inheritance and associations, and materialize
ownership before addressing self-description. Broader aligned language/tool studies
can then assess reuse beyond the single published metamodel. These extensions
build on the current foundation; none is implied by static conformance alone.
