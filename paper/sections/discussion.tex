\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}


\subsection{Related Work}
\paragraph{Formal conformance and MOF semantics.}
Paige et al. distinguish formal BON conformance and multiview consistency
from fidelity to the intended metamodel \cite{paige_brooke_ostroff_2007}.
Boronat and Meseguer establish executable MOF conformance with set, bag and
sequence semantics \cite{boronat_meseguer_2010}. Their MOF~2.0 EMOF/OCL account
restricts lower bounds to zero/one, upper bounds to one/unlimited, and requires
opposites for composition \cite[pp.~66,91]{boronat_meseguer_report}; VL-MOF permits general
finite bounds and unpaired composition. These compare stated subsets, not what
additional constraints the earlier framework could express.

Our contribution connects a structural subset of EMOF to checker equivalence on
raw inputs and correspondence between independent source satisfaction and
elaboration. Coverage equivalence or greater expressiveness would require a
translation between formalisms, which we do not establish.

\paragraph{Containment and model changes.}
K\"ohler et al. constrain incoming containment edges and cycles in instance
graphs and derive preservation conditions for graph transformations
\cite{koehler_lewin_taentzer_2007}. VL-MOF distinguishes repeated occurrences
of a link from distinct parent identities, and checks active reverse container
properties separately. This choice matters for nonunique composite features.
Coq4MDE proves preservation for ISC model composition, with injectivity and
freshness conditions relevant to multiplicities \cite[pp.~115--116]{kezadri_etal_2014}.
Its opposite condition compares membership, whereas ours compares occurrence
counts, with the mixed-uniqueness restriction stated in Section~\ref{sec:conformance}.
These differences do not establish greater standards fidelity.

\paragraph{Mechanized modeling tools.}
Isabelle/HOL DSL construction through conservative transformations
\cite{brucker_tuong_wolff_2020} and CoqTL certification tactics
\cite{cohen_tisi_douence_2024} address obligations different from checker and
AST-elaboration equivalence. MetaMorph formalizes four metamodeling layers
\cite{doller_2022}; our scope is structural conformance. NEREUS \cite{favre_duarte_2016} and KM3 \cite{jouault_bezivin_2006} offer
formal/textual metamodel specification, while Emfatic \cite{emfatic_docs} and
Xcore \cite{xcore_docs} provide textual Ecore; our named
notation has a proved connection from independent source semantics to Core.

\subsection{Threats to Validity}
\paragraph{Construct validity.}
Proofs concern our predicates; EMOF correspondence rests on the source argument.
The subset assumes complete finite snapshots and an implicit outer scope, and
restricts mixed-uniqueness opposites (Section~\ref{sec:conformance}). EMF agreement
does not establish standards fidelity.

\paragraph{Internal validity.}
Proofs start at AST or Core; tests cover selected parsing, JSON, bridge and
measurement cases, without proving general interchange/default behavior or
historical-run correctness. Ten warmups do not establish JIT stabilization.
Historical generation/orchestration sources lack hashes, commands include a dirty
tree, and hardware provenance is incomplete. Retained inputs and samples support
recomputing summaries, not reproducing the historical build.

\paragraph{External and conclusion validity.}
Coverage excludes Real, structured datatypes, operations and reflection,
default/unset histories, comments/tags and richer derived/read-only behavior;
static features and derived unions are unrepresented. Results concern this
structural subset, not full EMOF.

Train is the only public positive family; unchanged EMF Compare is outside the
bridge subset. Synthetic workloads expose structural sensitivity, not industrial
scalability. Results apply to the specified generic validators. Independent
process summaries, ranges and timeouts describe these workloads without
isolating bottlenecks or establishing complexity bounds.
