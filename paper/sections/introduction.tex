\section{Introduction}
A model-processing pipeline can change conformance by binding the wrong
declaration, collapsing repeated references or omitting inherited constraints.
EMOF makes these boundaries consequential: declaration identity differs from
spelling, a diamond exposes one inherited declaration along several paths, and
opposite ends constrain relationship occurrence counts.

We develop VL-MOF, a Lean 4 formalization of a stated static structural fragment
of EMOF 2.5.1. Its contract is a source-audited interpretation of the standard,
not a claim to implement all of EMOF. The artifact separates a symbolic source
model, independently defined source satisfaction, an identity-based finite Core,
declarative Core conformance, and an executable checker. This separation permits
proofs about actual elaboration rather than defining source validity to mean
that elaboration passes the checker.

The delivered adequacy result states that, under explicit lexical alias
conditions, a source document satisfies its independent semantics exactly when
it elaborates successfully to an accepted Core snapshot. Checker
acceptance is equivalent to Core conformance for every represented input,
including malformed raw records. The development also proves finite-closure
completeness, preserves occurrence counts and ordering through binding, and uses
the same checker for a bounded metadata interpretation pilot. A trusted Java
adapter exercises a restricted Ecore/XMI boundary through real EMF loading and
fresh resource construction.

Executable MOF semantics, mechanized conformance preservation and textual
metamodel languages are established prior work
\cite{boronat_meseguer_2010,kezadri_etal_2014,jouault_bezivin_2006}.
Our contribution is the checked connection between a separately specified
symbolic meaning, actual elaboration and executable decisions for the stated
fragment. Neither a new surface notation nor Lean itself is the scientific
claim. The precise boundary matters: the Lean theorems do not certify the prose
interpretation, textual parser, JSON decoder or EMF adapter.

We organize the investigation around three questions. RQ1 asks whether identity,
inherited applicability and occurrence-sensitive constraints admit a coherent
finite semantics with a complete executable decision procedure. RQ2 asks which
semantic guarantees connect authored symbolic documents to that procedure.
RQ3 asks what a real EMF interchange experiment preserves, and which source
constructs require rejection or explicitly recorded adaptation.
