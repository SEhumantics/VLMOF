\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
Metamodels define a modeling language's abstract syntax: its kinds of elements,
properties and relationships. The Meta Object Facility (MOF), including its
Essential MOF (EMOF) compliance level, provides a common foundation
\cite{omg_mof_251}. To obtain a verified conformance checker, these requirements
must be expressed over a precise model representation and connected to an
executable decision procedure.

Formal conformance and executable MOF semantics have established precedents
\cite{boronat_meseguer_2010,paige_brooke_ostroff_2007}. Building on them, we focus
on connecting a structural subset of EMOF to independently defined named
models, verified translation and executable checking. This raises three questions:
\begin{description}
\item[RQ1.] How are the selected structural EMOF requirements represented as
conformance predicates, and what restrictions does this formalization impose?
\item[RQ2.] What guarantees connect symbolic declarations, formal conformance
and executable checking?
\item[RQ3.] Where does the implementation agree with a specified EMF validation
setup, and how does validation time vary across controlled workloads?
\end{description}

We present \textbf{VL-MOF}, a formalization in Lean \cite{demoura_ullrich_2021}
of a structural subset of EMOF, with executable model-conformance checking. First, we define metamodel well-formedness and finite
model conformance, documenting their source requirements and the formalization's
additional restrictions. Second, we prove checker equivalence and preservation of the
independent meaning of named declarations under explicit naming conditions.
Third, an adapted railway case study and controlled workloads evaluate
validation decisions, loaded values and checking cost.

The rest of the paper proceeds as follows. Section~\ref{sec:motivation} motivates
the work; Section~\ref{sec:formalization} presents the semantics and proofs;
Section~\ref{sec:tool} describes the tool; Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} evaluates
it; Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses related work and limitations; and
Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.
