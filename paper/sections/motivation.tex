\section{Motivation}\label{sec:motivation}
\input{figures/train}
We adapt the railway case study of the Train Benchmark
\cite{szarnyas_etal_2018} as a running example for structural conformance and
its evaluation. Figure~\ref{fig:train} shows the metamodel and the inheritance
hierarchy of its domain concepts. Regions contain track elements and sensors;
segments and switches form the track, and routes specify the sensors and switch
positions needed for travel. We use this domain structure to study conformance,
rather than the benchmark's query workload or railway-safety rules.

Checking this model requires a precise account of structural conformance.
A Route with fewer than two required sensors violates multiplicity, while a
switch position whose reverse route disagrees with \emph{Route.follows}
violates the opposite relation. EMOF describes such structural requirements
through definitions and usage rules, together with reused UML definitions
\cite{omg_mof_251}. Our task is to define that conformance relation for a stated structural subset and prove that the
checker accepts exactly the models satisfying it.

Multiplicity, uniqueness and containment illustrate the work involved. UML
relates multiplicity to collection size and distinguishes unique from nonunique
association ends \cite{omg_uml_25}; MOF separately restricts container
identity, active container properties and containment cycles
\cite{omg_mof_251}. Occurrence counts, equality, and parent identities and paths
must be combined. Section~\ref{sec:formalization} makes these obligations
explicit and states the restrictions of our representation.
