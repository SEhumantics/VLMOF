\section{Motivation}
The railway metamodel of Sz\'{a}rnyas et al. connects routes, switch positions,
switches and sensors \cite{szarnyas_etal_2018}. We use a structural projection
to ask what an edit must preserve. Its authored snapshot and textual translation
are supplied separately from the original Train Benchmark Xcore and generated
inputs; the task concerns structural modeling, not railway safety.

Figure~\ref{fig:train} retains two opposite pairs and a required sensor
multiplicity. Its source Xcore includes:
\begin{verbatim}
contains SwitchPosition[] follows opposite route
refers Sensor[2..*] requires
\end{verbatim}
The inverse route has upper bound one. RailwayElement and TrackElement
inheritance, Boolean/Integer attributes and Position's three literals remain.

Consider one route, one switch, one switch position and two sensors. Removing
one required sensor violates the lower bound. Removing only the position's
reverse route value breaks opposite consistency while preserving local upper
bounds. This represented inconsistency need not be reachable through an
inverse-maintaining editor.

The inherited \emph{id} is ordinary Integer data: equal values on distinct
objects are structurally admissible. Train's SwitchSet query additionally uses
route activity, entry-semaphore state and switch-position agreement. The
projection omits semaphores and does not evaluate that domain query.

The projection retains six of ten classes and one of two enumerations, omitting
outer containers/regions, segments, semaphores and their connections. The surviving
task connects inherited values, sensors, opposites and containment. Larger states
support evaluation; separate nonunique witnesses expose opposite constraints
that Train's own uniqueness flags would otherwise conceal.
