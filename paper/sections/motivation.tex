\section{Motivation}
A metamodel defines more than the shapes of stored objects. It constrains which
objects may be linked, how inherited properties apply, and which changes leave
a model valid. A formal semantics makes these obligations explicit. It also
supports two complementary uses: deciding whether a particular snapshot conforms,
and proving which edits preserve conformance or necessarily violate it.

We illustrate these uses with the Train Benchmark metamodel
\cite{szarnyas_etal_2018}. A Route requires at least two Sensors and contains
SwitchPositions. Each position belongs to one Route and refers to one Switch.
Figure~\ref{fig:train} shows these relationships from the published diagram.
Role names and multiplicities are placed next to the class at the corresponding
association end; the diamond marks composition at Route.

The executable example has one route, switch and position, two sensors,
abstract RailwayElement/TrackElement ancestors, inherited integer \emph{id},
and Position's three literals. Removing one sensor violates \emph{requires};
removing only the reverse \emph{route} value breaks opposite consistency.
Section~3 proves a general exclusion of duplicate links even for a nonunique
association; Train's own uniqueness flags already exclude them.

\paragraph{Published diagram and executable artifact.}
The sources differ. The published diagram gives \emph{route} and \emph{target}
multiplicity~1; the pinned v1.0 Xcore generates $0..1$ for both. We supply a
separate \texttt{route-switch-published.dsl} example with the displayed mandatory
bounds. The historical native fixtures and timing workloads retain v1.0's bounds.
A position detached from its route, with both opposite observations empty,
conforms under the optional bound but violates the mandatory one. The distinction
affects admitted snapshots, not the correctness of the general checker.

The example retains six of ten classes and one of two enumerations, omitting
regions, segments, semaphores and their links. Evaluation also uses a full
structural v1.0 profile. Train's domain queries, including SwitchSet, require
further semaphore and switch conditions; structural conformance alone does not
establish those queries or railway safety.
